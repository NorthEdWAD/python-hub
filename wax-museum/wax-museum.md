Teaching the difference between **parameters** (the placeholders in the function definition) and **arguments** (the actual values passed in) can be surprisingly tricky because the terms are often used interchangeably in casual conversation.

To get your high schoolers moving and thinking, here are two kinesthetic activities to bridge that gap.

---

## 1. The "Label & Pass" Mailbox
This activity uses physical objects to represent the "scope" of a function. It helps students see that parameters live *inside* the function, while arguments come from the *outside*.

* **Setup:** Give one student a **manila folder** (the Function). On the inside of the folder, write a simple instruction like `print(Name + " is " + Age)`. 
* **The Parameters:** On the *outside* of the folder, cut two slots (mail slots) and label them **Name** and **Age**. These are your **parameters**—they are just empty slots waiting for input.
* **The Arguments:** Give another student two **index cards**. Ask them to write a specific name and a specific number on them (e.g., "Alice" and "17"). These are the **arguments**.
* **The Action:** The second student "calls" the function by sliding the cards into the slots. 
* **The Check:** The "Function" student opens the folder, takes the cards, and reads the completed instruction aloud.

> **Key Takeaway:** The labels on the folder slots (parameters) never change, but the cards being dropped in (arguments) change every time the function is called.

---

## 2. The "Human Logic" Proxy
This activity turns students into variables to demonstrate how values are assigned from the call to the definition.

* **Setup:** Designate two students to stand at the front of the room inside a taped-off square labeled **"Function Definition."** * **The Parameters:** Give these two students signs to hold that say **`x`** and **`y`**. They are the parameters; they define what the function expects to receive.
* **The Arguments:** Ask two other students to stand at their desks (the "Main Program"). Give them signs with actual values, like **`10`** and **`5`**. 
* **The Action:** When you shout "Execute!", the students with the numbers must walk over and hand their numbers to the students with the letters. 
* **The Switch:** To check for understanding, change the arguments. Give the "Main Program" students new numbers (e.g., `100` and `2`). Have them repeat the walk.
